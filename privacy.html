Privacy notice

Last updated 25 October 2026. This notice follows South Africa’s Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA).

In short

SDA Sabbath runs in your browser. It has no accounts, no adverts and no tracking, and its maker does not receive your plans, settings, pictures or anything else you do in it. Almost everything stays on the device you use. A few features send a request to another company, only when you press the button for them. They are listed below.

Who is responsible

SDA Sabbath is free software made by Leo McQuire and released under the MIT licence. The source is at github.com/LeeMcQ/Church_Service. Questions about this notice can be raised there under “Issues”. The church or person who installs and runs SDA Sabbath is responsible for any personal information they type into it (for example names in a service plan or on a banner).

What stays on your device

Your settings, service plans, banners and pictures, imported Bibles and hymnbooks, hymn and video lists, the church details you enter (name, town, service time, giving link) and, if you choose to keep it, your AI key are stored in this browser’s own storage (IndexedDB and localStorage). They are not uploaded. You can erase everything by clearing this site’s data in your browser, or with “Download a fresh copy” and “Reset” in Settings. Backups you export are files you keep yourself; they never include your AI key.

The SDA Sabbath cloud

Accounts, usage statistics and feedback are kept in a database run for SDA Sabbath by Supabase (a hosting company; the data centre region is chosen by the maintainer). Only the maintainer can see the statistics and messages; a signed-in person can see their own messages and the replies. Statistics are used only to understand how many churches use SDA Sabbath, how much, and which parts, so it can be improved. They are kept for as long as the app is in use by that church, and the daily counts are summarised per day.

You can switch statistics off at any time in Settings → Account (or in the first-start setup). Settings → Account → Delete my account deletes your account, your messages and the statistics of the devices you signed in on. To have the statistics of a device deleted without an account, send its id (shown in Settings → Account) through Comments and suggestions.

Why, and your consent

Each of these is used only to give you the feature you asked for, and only when you use it. Using the feature is your consent to that request. Usage statistics are explained in the first-start setup and can be switched off there or in Settings → Account; the maintainer relies on a legitimate interest in keeping SDA Sabbath working and improving it. You can stop at any time by switching statistics off, signing out, removing your AI key in Settings, or going offline.

Please do not type personal details into AI buttons

Names, phone numbers, addresses and health or other private details should not be typed into a request that goes to an AI provider, and should be kept out of banners and plans that are shown to or shared with others. Photographs of people need their permission under POPIA.

Your rights

Most information is on your own device: you can see, change and delete it yourself at any time. For the SDA Sabbath cloud you can ask, through Comments and suggestions, to see or correct what is kept about you, or delete your account yourself in Settings → Account. You may also ask the church that runs SDA Sabbath what it keeps about you. If you believe your personal information has been handled wrongly, you may complain to the Information Regulator (South Africa): inforegulator.org.za.

Children

SDA Sabbath is a tool for church media teams. It does not knowingly collect information from children.

Security

An AI key kept in the browser can be read by anyone who can use that browser profile, and by other pages on the same website address. Use a key with a low spending limit, choose “Forget the key when this window closes” on a shared computer, and remove the key after the service.

Changes

If this notice changes, the date at the top changes and the app’s Settings points here.
